## Why Fretboard Companion?

Most chord charts show you a shape and stop there. Fretboard Companion helps you see **where** a chord lives on the neck, **how** it sounds and **why** it works, all on one screen.

It is made for:

- **Beginners** learning their first chords and wondering what "major", "minor" and "7th" actually mean.
- **Self-taught players** who know a few shapes and want to connect them across the whole neck.
- **Teachers and students** who need a quick visual to point at during a lesson.

## What you can do

### See the chord
- A **chord diagram** you can switch between **finger numbers**, **note names** and **intervals**, with fret numbers and position dots.
- **Rotate** the diagram to a horizontal player view, or **tilt** it in 3D.
- A full **fretboard** showing every place the chord's notes appear on the first 15 frets. Bright dots are the chord shape, faded dots are the same notes elsewhere, and orange marks the root.
- **Tilt view** angles the neck like you are looking down at your own guitar, and each note lights up in place with a ripple as it sounds.

### Hear the chord
- **Tap any note** to hear it.
- Press **Strum** or **Arpeggio** to hear the whole chord.
- Sound is synthesized live in your browser (Karplus-Strong plucked string on the Web Audio API). No audio files, no downloads.

### Understand the chord
- The **chord formula** and what each note contributes.
- Plain-language explanations of scales, major vs minor, 7th, sus, 6th and power chords, and barre chords.
- **Where it lives in a key**: see which keys the chord belongs to and jump straight to its neighbours.

### Play it anywhere on the neck
- The five **CAGED shapes** (C, A, G, E, D) show the same chord in five positions, each named after the open chord it comes from.

### Find more voicings
- Under the chord, a **More voicings** grid shows every playable way to play the chord: open chords, barre chords, 5–6 string shapes, 4-string and 3-string versions, all the way up the neck. Tap one to see it on the neck and hear it.
- Filter by size (5–6, 4, or 3 or fewer strings) and optionally include **inversions** (the 3rd or 5th in the bass instead of the root). Picking a letter under **Shape** returns to the five classic CAGED shapes.
- Voicings are generated by rules, not typed in: only chord tones, every needed note present, inside a 4-fret stretch, no muted string in the middle, and a fingering a real hand can play (see below). `tools/validate-more-voicings.js` re-checks all of them.
- Shapes and voicings that are too hard to play are left out. A greyed-out letter under **Shape** means that shape is too hard at that position; the app switches to the nearest playable one.

### How the app decides a chord is playable
There is no official standard for this, because it depends on hand size and technique. The app uses a short list of limits of a real hand (`whyHard()` in `js/app.js`): at most four fingers, everything inside a 4-fret stretch, one finger per fret, no finger lying across an open string, only the index and ring finger can lie across several strings (the pinky only across two neighbours, the middle finger never), a maximum reach for each pair of fingers, and no "full 4-fret stretch with two fingers lying flat" (which no real chart uses). It also skips fingers lying across a muted string, which is an advanced trick.

The limits were calibrated against real chord charts: the open [chords-db](https://github.com/tombatossals/chords-db) database (MIT license). 97.2% of its 818 fingerings for the chord types in this app pass (a fingering counts as passing if the chart's own finger numbers pass, or the app can find another fingering for the same shape); the rest are rare or advanced, and a few are typos in the database. To re-run the check yourself:

```bash
npm i --no-save @tombatossals/chords-db
node tools/check-against-chords-db.js js/app.js
```

This is a calibration, not proof: chords-db is a community collection, and "comfortable" differs between people. If a chord is wrongly hidden or wrongly shown, please open an issue with the chord and the fingering.

### Find scales
- A **Chords / Scales** switch in the sidebar. In Scales mode the fretboard shows every note of the scale, labelled with note names or scale degrees.
- 12 scales: major, natural minor, major and minor pentatonic, blues, the five other modes (dorian, phrygian, lydian, mixolydian, locrian), harmonic minor and melodic minor.
- **Positions**: pick one "box" at a time (5 for pentatonic scales, 7 for the others) to learn the neck piece by piece. Play the scale up or down and watch the notes ripple.
- **Chords from this scale**: the triads and 7th chords built on each note, with Roman numerals. Tap one to jump to it in Chords mode.

### Play along with a progression
- A third view, **Progression**: pick a key, choose a preset (pop, classic, canon, jazz ii–V–I, 12-bar blues, two minor ones) or build your own from the key's chords.
- Press **Play** (or the space bar) to hear it in time with a metronome click. Set the tempo, loop it, and choose how it is played: once per chord, every beat, or as an arpeggio.
- The player picks the hand positions for you so your hand travels as little as possible, and the fretboard and chord diagram follow along with a "next chord" hint.

### Learn to read tablature
- A fourth view, **Tab**, replaces the fretboard with a tab trainer. It hides the neck on purpose: the tab is the lesson.
- **Learn**: 14 techniques, each shown as a drawn tab and as the plain-text tab you find on websites, with how to play it, what it should sound like and a common mistake. Hammer-on, pull-off, slide up and down, slide into / off a note, dead note, vibrato, bend, pre-bend, bend and release, palm mute, natural harmonic, tapping and trill. "Another example" gives a new random one; Play lights up each note as it sounds, and you can change the tempo.
- **Licks**: four short phrases that combine techniques (blues-rock opener, slide and hammer groove, palm-muted riff, tapping and trill) to practise slowly and speed up.
- **Quiz**: name the technique from a drawn tab, from a text tab, or by ear, with score and streak.
- **On the neck**: under every tab there is a close-up of the fretting hand on a few frets. Fingers drop, lift, slide or bend the string in time with the sound, with suggested finger numbers and a one-line instruction for each moment. A pick marker flashes on picked notes and stays dark on hammer-ons, pull-offs, slides and taps, so you can see which notes are not picked. A dashed line shows how far a bend or vibrato should go, and the palm lights up for palm mutes. It is drawn from the same data and timings as the sound, not by hand. In the quiz it appears only after you answer, so it does not give the answer away.
- Hammer-ons, pull-offs and slides keep one ringing string and only change its pitch, so they sound like the real thing instead of two separate plucks.
- `tools/validate-tab.js` checks every generated example (valid strings and frets, notes that go the right way, drawing and text tab agree, the example really uses its technique) and the close-up at every moment (the right fingers are down, upper fingers lift on pull-offs, nothing is left behind by a slide, the string bends, the pick shows only on picked notes).

### Make it yours
- **Light and Night** themes. It follows your device on the first visit, then remembers your choice.
- Works on desktop, tablet and phone.

## Chords included

18 chord types on all twelve root notes:

- **Triads**: major, minor
- **Seventh chords**: dominant 7, major 7, minor 7
- **Suspended, 6th and power**: sus2, sus4, major 6, minor 6, power chord (5)
- **Diminished and augmented**: diminished (dim), diminished 7 (dim7), minor 7♭5 (m7♭5, half-diminished), augmented (aug)
- **Colour chords**: add9, dominant 9, minor 9 and minor-major 7 (written m(maj7), mM7 or m7M, for example `x02110` is Am(maj7))

The older types have all five CAGED shapes. For the newer ones, shapes are made from a classic shape by moving one or two notes and kept only if a hand can play them. Dominant 9, minor 9 and add9 have no classic shapes, so the app shows the generated voicings instead (the CAGED row is greyed out, and the first voicing is selected). In the interval view the 9th is labelled 9, the augmented 5th ♯5 and the diminished 7th ♭♭7.

There is no complete official list of chord fingerings. A chord is defined by its notes, and the app generates every fingering that has exactly the right notes and that a hand can play. [chords-db](https://github.com/tombatossals/chords-db) is the closest community reference, and `tools/check-against-chords-db.js` compares the generated voicings with it for every type.

## Run it yourself

**Runtime dependencies: none.** Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No framework, no `node_modules`, no build step.

**Browser:** any current Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari (needs the Web Audio API and CSS custom properties). Audio starts after your first click or tap, which is a browser rule.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/maikereis/fretboard-companion.git
cd fretboard-companion
```

Then pick one:

| Option | How |
| --- | --- |
| Open the file | Double-click `index.html` (works because the scripts are not ES modules) |
| Node.js 18+ | `npx serve` and open the printed address (`npx http-server` also works) |
| VS Code | Install **Live Server**, right-click `index.html`, **Open with Live Server** |

Nothing needs `npm install`; `npx` tools are downloaded temporarily and not added to the project.

### Project structure

### Troubleshooting

- **No sound:** click or tap a note first (browsers block audio until a user gesture), then check that your device is not muted.

### Your own tab ("My tab")

It opens with the melody of "Happy birthday to you" (key of G, four staves, 90 bpm) so there is something to play straight away.

Paste a plain-text tab (six lines, one per string) into **Tab → My tab** and the app shows, move by move, which finger goes where, then plays it.

- **Reads** numbers, `h` hammer-on, `p` pull-off, `/` `\\` slides (a mark glued to the note links two notes, a mark with dashes before the number is a slide-in), `b` bend, `pb` pre-bend, `r` release (in `5b7-7r5` the second 7 is the bent string being held, not a new pick; the release starts where the `r` sits), `~` vibrato, `x` dead note, `<12>` harmonic, `t5` tap, `tr7` trill and a `P.M.---|` line for palm muting.
- **Several staves:** paste as many six-line staves as you like, one after another or with blank lines or an “or” between them. Typographic dashes (—) are accepted, and a tab whose line breaks were lost when copying is cut back into lines.
- **Parts:** by default each staff is one part. You can also split by bars (`|`) or by gaps of three dashes or more. Play all parts or one at a time, with loop, metronome and a count-in. In "Follow along", click a note to put an orange bar where playback should start, and click a second note to mark where it should stop. Playing then covers only that section, and the loop turns on so it repeats. A third click starts over, and Clear removes the bars.
- **Rhythm and tempo:** a line such as `60 bpm` above the tab sets the tempo, and `rhythm 1/16` (or 1/4, 1/8, 1/32) sets the dash length. Timing either follows the spacing of the dashes or uses even steps.
- **Limits:** other symbols are skipped with a message. Tuning is read from the string names, but the close-up shows frets as written. Rhythm is taken from column spacing, not from written note values.
- `node tools/validate-tabtext.js js/app.js` checks that every lesson survives writing as text and reading back, and fuzzes the reader.
