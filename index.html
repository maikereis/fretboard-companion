Scales and chords in plain words

Semitone = one fret. Everything below is just counting frets.

Scale = a set of 7 notes that sound good together. The major scale steps up by 2,2,1,2,2,2,1 frets. From C that gives C D E F G A B.

Chord = notes stacked from a scale, skipping every other one (1st, 3rd, 5th). Do that from each of the 7 notes and you get 7 chords, one per scale degree. That is called a key. The Roman numerals (I, ii, iii…) are just the position of the chord in the key. Capitals are major, lowercase are minor.

Major vs minor: only the 3rd note changes. Major has a 3rd that is 4 frets above the root (bright). Minor has it 3 frets above (darker).

7th chords: add a 4th note on top. Major 7 adds a note 11 frets above the root (dreamy, jazzy). Dominant 7 (just “7”) adds a note 10 frets above, on a major chord (bluesy, wants to move to another chord). Minor 7 adds that same 10-fret note to a minor chord (smooth, mellow).

Sus, 6th and power chords: a sus2 or sus4 chord swaps the 3rd for the 2nd or 4th, so it is neither major nor minor and feels unresolved. A 6th chord adds the 6th note of the scale for a smooth, jazzy colour. A power chord is only the root and the 5th, with no 3rd, so it sounds strong and works over major or minor.

Progressions: a chord progression is a list of chords played one after another, usually repeating. Roman numerals name each chord by its place in the key, so I–V–vi–IV works in any key. In G it is G–D–Em–C, in C it is C–G–Am–F. Pick a key, choose a preset or build your own, and set the tempo.

Scales on the neck: a pentatonic scale has five notes and is the easiest place to start soloing. The blues scale adds one “blue note”. The modes (dorian, lydian…) are the major scale started from a different note, so each has its own mood. Use the Positions to learn the neck one box at a time, then join the boxes together.

Barre chords: when the shape slides up the neck, one finger (usually the index) lays across the strings. Moving the same shape up one fret raises the chord one semitone.
